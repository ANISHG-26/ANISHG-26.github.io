Platform Engineer · Builder · Experimenter

Before You Build Higher,
Make The Ground Dependable.

I take Platform Engineering quite literally. Build the foundation: a place to run, a path to deploy, and a way to understand what happens. Then help people build on it with confidence.

That idea reaches beyond work for me. I’m learning to approach growth in the same way: pay attention to the basics, test my assumptions, and make the next step something I can build on.

How I Try To Work

Good foundations are technical. They are also human.

01 / Empathy

Build For People

Understand where developers lose time or context. Make the next change easier to follow, and the next failure easier to understand.

02 / Integrity

Keep It Honest

Be clear about what I built, what we built together, and what is still an experiment. Useful engineering starts with an accurate picture.

03 / Curiosity

Keep Building

Ask why. Try something small. Learn from what happens. Reflection matters most when it helps me make the next version better.

The Tools Changed. The Instinct Stayed.

01 / Delivery

Make The Path Repeatable

At Accenture, reusable CI/CD and onboarding automation brought me closer to the engineers using the platform. The recurring tasks were often the best place to start.

02 / Reproducibility

Make The Environment Understandable

At Morgan Stanley, containerizing legacy tests meant tracing dependencies and making execution repeatable. Before making something faster, make it possible to reason about.

03 / Foundations

Give The Team Something To Build On

At EY, that instinct expanded into Kubernetes delivery, secure release workflows, shared artifacts, and observability. The work connected infrastructure choices with the developer’s next step.
